# Partner Engine

> Every market has somewhere between fifty and two hundred people who already hold the trust you are trying to build from zero. Renting five minutes of that trust is cheaper than manufacturing an hour of your own.

## The question this answers

- Paid acquisition cost is climbing and organic growth is slow — who already has the audience we need, and how do we get in front of it?
- Should we pay this podcast or newsletter for a placement, or try to earn a mention instead?
- We keep pitching partnerships cold and getting ignored — what is missing?
- How many of these relationships do we actually need before this becomes a channel, not a favor?

## When to run it

Run this when your buyer is concentrated enough to name the podcasts, newsletters, communities, or individuals they already trust — a market with visible congregation points, not one atomized across the open web. It works fastest when your addressable market is under a few thousand accounts, and more slowly but still viably at consumer scale, where the "audience" is a creator's following rather than an industry association.

**Do not run this when** you cannot name at least twenty real targets today — if the list requires guessing, the market is too diffuse for this method; use `gtm/lead-engine` or `gtm/audience-engine` instead. Do not run it as a first move with zero warm capital: a founder with no product, no case study, and no existing relationship of any kind will spend the 30-day entry period in Move 2 on threads that a paid channel would have closed for less money.

## The method

### Move 1 — Build the list before you build the pitch

Name 50 to 100 specific targets across at least three categories — podcasts and newsletters, individual creators or operators with a following, and associations or communities with a membership list. A category with zero entries means you have not looked hard enough, not that the category does not apply here. For each target, record reach, engagement (reply rate and comment quality beat follower count), and who actually decides — an editor, an agency, or the person themselves.

**Exit criterion:** a ranked list of 50+ named targets with a decision-maker identified for each, not a list of platforms or categories.

### Move 2 — Spend 30 days earning attention before asking for anything

Comment with substance, share without tagging yourself for credit, reference their work accurately in your own. The point is to exist in the target's memory before the first ask arrives. Skipping this step is the most common failure in this method — a cold sponsorship pitch to someone who has never heard of you converts at rates indistinguishable from a cold email blast, because you have bought nothing but their inbox.

**Threshold:** if a target has registered zero genuine interaction with you after 30 days, either they were not worth the list or the interaction was not genuine — check which before moving to Move 3.

### Move 3 — Split the list by earn-vs-buy, not by preference

Every target sits on a spectrum from high-affinity/lower-reach to low-affinity/higher-reach. Earn the first: propose an interview, a co-created piece, or a named affiliate arrangement tied to a real commission, not a link nobody tracks. Buy the second: a sponsorship slot, a newsletter insertion, or a targeted placement priced like media, because affinity with you is not there yet to substitute for it.

**Decision rule:** if the target's audience overlaps yours by more than half and Move 2 already produced some warmth, earn it. If reach is large but affinity is near zero, buying is faster and more honest about what you are actually purchasing — access, not endorsement.

### Move 4 — Price the earned placements like a channel, not a favor

An earned interview or co-created piece still costs the target's time and reputational risk, so bring something that makes saying yes easy: a specific angle their audience has not heard, a data point that is yours alone, or an offer to do the production work. Track results the way you would a paid channel — a unique link, a promo code, attributed signups — so "we did a podcast" becomes a number instead of an anecdote.

**Exit criterion:** at least three earned placements with attributable results, so the next round of asks can cite proof instead of hope.

### Move 5 — Compare the two lanes on the same unit economics

Run buy and earn side by side against the same acquisition-cost math your other channels use: fully loaded cost — including the hours spent on Move 2 — against the customers or qualified conversations produced. Partner-sourced customers often carry a lower churn rate because they arrive pre-vouched; if you are not tracking that separately, you are underpricing the channel.

**Threshold:** if partner-sourced CAC beats your next-best channel and the relationship survives one renewal cycle, formalize it — a standing arrangement, not a one-off ask — and move the target from the list to a managed partner roster.

## Failure modes

**The cold ask disguised as warmth** — a single comment on someone's post, followed immediately by a pitch. The tell is a reply rate indistinguishable from cold outreach. → Hold the line on 30 real days in Move 2; track interactions, do not estimate them.

**Borrowed audience, no attribution** — three podcast appearances happen and nobody can say whether any of them produced a customer. → Every placement gets a unique link or code before it airs, no exceptions.

**Category monoculture** — the entire list is podcasts because that is the founder's favorite medium to consume. → Enforce the three-category minimum in Move 1; the buyer's actual habits decide the mix, not yours.

**Partnership as unpaid marketing department** — a partner is asked for a placement, an intro, a co-webinar, and a testimonial, all unpaid, because the relationship is "warm." → Price earned asks like the labor they are; a partner who feels used stops being one.

**The list that never gets worked** — 80 targets get named in a spreadsheet once and nobody owns outreach after week one. → Assign one owner and a weekly quota of new interactions from Move 2; an unworked list is a research project, not a channel.

## Outputs

- A ranked list of 50-100 named targets across at least three categories, with reach, engagement, and decision-maker identified.
- A 30-day engagement log per target, dated and specific.
- An earn-vs-buy classification with the rationale for each target.
- Attributed results per placement: unique link or code, signups, and qualified conversations.
- A partner-sourced CAC comparison against your primary paid channel.
- A managed partner roster for arrangements that cleared the Move 5 threshold.

## Lineage

Chet Holmes's Dream 100 method in *The Ultimate Sales Machine* argued that any market has a small, nameable set of people who control access to the buyers you want, and that systematically working that list beats broad prospecting. The core claim holds; digital marketers since have adapted it with a shift toward affiliate and content partnerships that Holmes's original B2B sales-team version did not anticipate.

This articulation departs from the source by separating "earn" from "buy" as a first-class decision rather than treating every relationship the same, and by requiring the same unit-economics discipline used for paid channels — Holmes's original describes the courtship and skips the measurement, which is how the method degenerates into busywork in practice.
